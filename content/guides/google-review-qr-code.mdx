## Why Google Reviews Still Decide Who Gets Chosen

When someone searches "coffee shop near me" or "best tacos downtown," Google's local results lean heavily on star rating and how recently reviews came in. A business sitting at 4.5+ stars with reviews from the last few weeks reads as active and trustworthy — and that's usually who gets the click, the call, or the walk-in.

The problem isn't that customers are unhappy. It's that leaving a review takes effort most people don't bother with once they've left your building. A **Google review QR code** removes almost all of that effort: someone scans it while still sitting at the table or standing at the register, and they're one tap away from a rating.

Most businesses already have plenty of satisfied customers walking out the door every day — the review link generator step is really about capturing that goodwill at the exact moment it exists, before it fades on the drive home.

## Review QR Codes vs. Feedback QR Codes

These two get confused often, so it's worth separating them clearly:

- **Review QR code** — public, goes straight to your Google listing, visible to anyone searching for your business
- **Feedback QR code** — private, lands only in your dashboard, useful for catching problems before they turn into a public complaint

Many venues run both side by side: a review QR code at checkout for the general flow, and a feedback QR code on the table for anyone who wants to flag something in the moment. Neither one should be used to filter _who_ gets asked to review you — that's the part Google's policy actually cares about. What you're allowed to do is give unhappy customers an _additional_ private option, not remove their ability to post publicly.

## Getting More Scans, Not Just More QR Codes

Printing a QR code and hoping for the best rarely works as well as being deliberate about where and when customers see it. A few things that consistently make a difference:

- **Timing matters more than design.** A code handed over with the check, right after a good experience, outperforms one buried on a wall poster nobody looks at.
- **Say what it does.** A short line of text — "Scan to leave us a review" — removes any hesitation about what happens after the scan.
- **Keep it to one action per code.** A QR code that tries to be a menu, a review link, and a loyalty signup all at once usually confuses people into doing none of them.
- **Track your scans.** Knowing whether your review QR code is actually getting scanned — and from where — tells you if it needs a better spot, not just a redesign.
- **Refresh the placement occasionally.** A code that's been sitting in the same spot for a year tends to fade into the background; moving it to a new touchpoint can revive scan rates.

None of this requires a big campaign — it's mostly about putting the code in front of people at the moment they're already feeling good about the visit, and getting out of their way after that.
