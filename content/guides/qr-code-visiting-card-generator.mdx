## Static vCard vs. Dynamic Digital Visiting Card: Which Should You Choose?

Before generating your card's code, it's essential to understand the two primary architectures available for visiting card QR codes:

| Feature | Static vCard (Offline) | Dynamic Digital Profile (Online) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **How It Works** | Contact data encoded directly inside the matrix | QR points to an interactive online bio-page |
| **Internet Dependency** | **Works completely offline** | Requires mobile data / WiFi to load page |
| **Post-Print Editing** | Cannot be changed after printing | **Editable anytime without reprinting** |
| **Data Capacity** | Name, 1-2 phones, email, org, basic URL | Full bio, social links, calendar, PDF resume, video |
| **Scan Analytics** | None | Real-time scan counts, geographic locations, devices |
| **Matrix Density** | Moderate to dense (more dots) | Ultra-clean & simple (faster scan at small sizes) |
| **Best For** | Stable personal contact info, permanent executives | Freelancers, agencies, sales teams, changing roles |

<Callout type="info" title="Density Matters on Printed Cards">
Static vCards store every character of your name, email, and address in the code pattern. To keep the QR pattern clean and easy to scan at small dimensions (under 1 inch), include only your essential contact fields (Full Name, Primary Mobile, Work Email, Company, and Website).
</Callout>

## Visiting Card Print & Layout Best Practices

Printing a QR code on a compact 3.5 × 2.0 inch card requires careful attention to dimensions, contrast, and paper finishing:

### 1. Sizing and Proportions
- **Standard Minimum Size:** Never print your visiting card QR code smaller than **0.8 × 0.8 inches (20 × 20 mm)**.
- **With Embedded Logo:** If your QR code has a center logo, maintain a minimum dimension of **0.95 to 1.0 inch (24 to 25 mm)**.
- **Quiet Zone Margin:** Ensure at least 3mm to 4mm of empty space surrounds the outer perimeter of the QR code so nearby text or design borders don't confuse camera auto-focus.

### 2. High Contrast and Color Schemes
- Always place dark QR modules (e.g., `#111111`, deep navy, dark forest green) against a light background (`#FFFFFF` or soft cream `#F5F1EC`).
- **Avoid Inverted QR Codes:** Never print a white QR code on a dark black/brown card. Many legacy Android camera sensors struggle to decode inverted contrast codes.

### 3. Paper Finishing & Coatings
- **Matte or Silk Cardstock (Recommended):** Diffuses ambient office and conference room lighting, eliminating specular glare when scanned.
- **Glossy Finish (Caution):** High-gloss UV coatings can reflect overhead fluorescent lighting directly into phone lenses, causing scanning delays.
- **Spot UV & Foil Stamping:** If using luxury gold foil or spot gloss, apply it to your company name and logo, **not** to the functional QR code modules.
