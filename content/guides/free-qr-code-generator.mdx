### The Mathematics Behind Logo Embedding: Reed-Solomon Error Correction

How does a smartphone camera still read a QR code when the center is blocked by a graphic? 

QR codes incorporate **Reed-Solomon Error Correction** algorithms. This mathematical redundancy reconstructs missing or damaged data modules within the pattern. When you gen qrcode online with an embedded image, you must select the appropriate error correction level:

| Error Correction Level | Redundancy Capacity | Recommended Center Image Area | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level L (Low)** | ~7% recovery | Not recommended for images | Plain URLs, maximum data capacity |
| **Level M (Medium)** | ~15% recovery | Small icon (under 10% of total area) | Standard web codes with minimal graphics |
| **Level Q (Quartile)** | ~25% recovery | Medium logo (15%–20% of total area) | Balanced branding on printed marketing materials |
| **Level H (High)** | ~30% recovery | Bold logo / centered image (20%–28% area) | **Best for custom image-in-QR generators** |

<Callout type="warning" title="Always Use Level H When Embedding Images">
When uploading a custom logo or image, always select **Level H (High Error Correction)**. This guarantees that up to 30% of the pattern can be occupied by the image without risking scan failures in low light or at odd angles.
</Callout>

### 4 Golden Rules for Custom Image QR Codes

1. **Keep the Image Symmetrical and Centered:** Place the image in the exact mathematical center of the matrix. Avoid covering the three primary position detection squares (the large corner eye patterns).
2. **Add a Clear Buffer Zone (Quiet Island):** Maintain a slight solid border or padding around your center image so individual QR dots don't touch the edge of your graphic.
3. **Preserve High Contrast:** Ensure your foreground dots (black or dark brand colors) strongly contrast against the background (white or light cream). Avoid inverted colors where the background is darker than the QR modules.
4. **Test Across Multiple Devices:** Scan your newly generated code with both an iPhone (Apple Camera app) and an Android device (Google Lens) before sending it to the printer.

## Essential Print & Display Checklist

Before sending your custom QR codes to production, run through this practical pre-flight checklist:

<Callout type="info" title="The 10:1 Scanning Distance Rule">
As a general rule of thumb, the ratio of scanning distance to QR code size should be approximately **10:1**. A QR code intended to be scanned from 10 inches away should be at least 1 inch wide; a code on a poster scanned from 5 feet (60 inches) away should be at least 6 inches wide.
</Callout>

- [ ] **Contrast Check:** Is the foreground significantly darker than the background? (Never print light gray on white or dark blue on black).
- [ ] **Quiet Zone:** Is there a minimum blank margin of at least 4 modules around the entire outer perimeter of the code?
- [ ] **Image Proportions:** Is the center logo taking up no more than 25%–30% of the total surface area?
- [ ] **Error Correction:** Is the error correction level set to **H (High)**?
- [ ] **Physical Test Scan:** Have you printed a 100% scale proof on regular paper and tested it under natural lighting with at least two different smartphone cameras?
